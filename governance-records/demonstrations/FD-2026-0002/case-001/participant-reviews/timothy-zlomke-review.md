## What an Independent Demonstration Taught Me About Building Constitutional AI

I participated in the TA-14 registration and demonstration process to determine
whether Harmonic's constitutional runtime could be evaluated through an attributable,
version-frozen, evidence-based process rather than through discussion alone.

Several aspects of the process stood out to me.

First, registration and demonstration remained distinct throughout. Registering
Harmonic established an attributable identity and a declared baseline, but it did
not constitute validation, certification, or acceptance of Harmonic's technical
claims. That separation was maintained consistently.

Second, requiring a frozen implementation before demonstration proved valuable.
Once the implementation was frozen, I could not modify the runtime to address
observations that emerged during evaluation. While that required discipline, it
also gave me confidence that the demonstration reflected what the software actually
did rather than what I wished it had done.

The most valuable outcome for me was not whether Harmonic produced a particular
runtime determination. It was the distinction that emerged between demonstrating
the runtime's constitutional behavior and independently establishing the surrounding
institutional chronology.

The evidence supported that the frozen runtime reconstructed the constitutional
state represented within the submitted packet and produced its own determination.

At the same time, the evidence did not independently establish the complete
institutional sequence outside the runtime itself, including:

- the pre-change authority state;
- the attributable authority revocation event;
- the independently preserved post-change state; and
- an external record confirming the execution did not occur.

I appreciated that TA-14 neither treated those records as though they existed nor
converted the runtime demonstration into a broader finding than the evidence
supported.

Preserving that distinction increased my confidence in the integrity of the process.

That finding directly influenced the subsequent engineering work on Harmonic.

Rather than modifying the frozen implementation, I began a separate Version 2
lineage focused on preserving independently attributable institutional evidence
objects, constitutional state snapshots, constitutional receipts, execution
outcomes, replay artifacts, and evidence bundles that compose with the runtime
rather than becoming part of it.

From my perspective, that is how an independent demonstration should function.

It should not merely confirm what works.

It should identify the evidentiary boundaries of what has actually been demonstrated
and allow subsequent engineering to respond without rewriting history.

I also appreciated that the process preserved separate voices.

TA-14 published what the admitted evidence supported.

I retained the ability to describe what I learned from participating.

Those are complementary responsibilities.

Overall, I found the process rigorous, fair, and valuable.

It strengthened my confidence in preserving versioned evidence, freezing
implementations before consequential evaluation, and allowing independent findings
to shape future engineering rather than retroactively altering the work that was
evaluated.

**Timothy E. Zlomke**  
Founder, Moral Clarity AI
