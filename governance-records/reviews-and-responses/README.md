# TA-14 Reviews, Responses & Governed Messaging Record

## TA-14 Authority Governance Institution

The TA-14 Reviews, Responses & Governed Messaging Record defines the institutional
architecture for preserving attributable reviews, responses, challenges, corrections,
technical commentary, external publications, replication requests, demonstration
requests, and governed communications associated with records inside the TA-14 AI
Governance Exchange.

This record exists to preserve independent voices without collapsing those voices
into TA-14 findings, participant declarations, negotiated narratives, or silently
rewritten historical records.

The objective is not to create another public comment feed.

The objective is to create an inspectable governance history.

---

# 1. Governing Purpose

The TA-14 AI Governance Exchange is intended to preserve more than registration
records.

A mature governed record may include:

Registered Baseline  
→ Governance Profile  
→ Frozen Implementation  
→ Governed Demonstration  
→ Admitted Evidence  
→ Runtime Artifact  
→ TA-14 Finding  
→ Participant Review  
→ Participant Response  
→ Independent Review  
→ Evidence Challenge  
→ Factual Correction  
→ Technical Comment  
→ External Publication  
→ Engineering Learning  
→ New Version  
→ New Registered Baseline  
→ Future Demonstration

Each stage must remain distinguishable.

Each voice must remain attributable.

Each later record must preserve the chronology that came before it.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

## Preserve the beginning.

A governance entity should establish its attributable baseline before TA-14 review,
demonstration, comparison, challenge, or other consequential institutional interaction.

The starting record should preserve, where applicable:

- entity identity;
- architecture name;
- version;
- stewardship;
- organization;
- jurisdiction;
- claims;
- non-claims;
- limitations;
- evidence;
- authority;
- provenance;
- declared boundaries;
- publication permissions;
- relevant rights.

Later interpretation must not erase earlier state.

---

# 3. Core Institutional Rule

The TA-14 Reviews, Responses & Governed Messaging system operates under the following
rule:

**Later records may add context, challenge conclusions, correct objective errors,
introduce new evidence, or document subsequent learning. They must not silently
rewrite the original record.**

Participant response does not equal TA-14 endorsement.

TA-14 finding does not require participant agreement.

Independent review does not automatically modify an existing TA-14 finding.

Technical commentary does not become evidence merely because it is published.

A message does not become a finding merely because TA-14 received it.

Missing evidence must not be reconstructed retrospectively merely to improve a
previous demonstration record.

---

# 4. Separate Voices Doctrine

The TA-14 Authority Governance Institution preserves separate institutional voices.

TA-14 publishes what admitted evidence supports.

Participants may publish what they experienced, learned, disputed, or concluded.

Independent reviewers may examine TA-14 findings, participant claims, evidence
packages, demonstrations, or related records.

Challengers may identify specific evidentiary deficiencies.

Technical contributors may submit bounded technical observations.

The public should be able to inspect those records without the voices being merged
into one negotiated narrative.

The objective is not consensus.

The objective is attributable institutional continuity.

---

# 5. Record Classes

The TA-14 AI Governance Exchange should support the following governed record classes.

---

## 5.1 Participant Review

A Participant Review is an attributable statement submitted by the steward,
architect, founder, owner, authorized participant, or other recognized representative
associated with a registered governance entity or governed demonstration.

Participant Reviews may describe:

- registration experience;
- baseline preservation;
- version freezing;
- evidence admission;
- demonstration conduct;
- factual-record review;
- agreement or disagreement with findings;
- process integrity;
- lessons learned;
- engineering implications;
- subsequent version development;
- institutional observations;
- evidence limitations;
- governance maturity.

Participant Reviews remain the participant's own voice.

They are not:

- TA-14 findings;
- TA-14 certifications;
- TA-14 endorsements;
- TA-14 validations;
- legal conclusions;
- regulatory approvals;
- ownership determinations;
- technical guarantees.

A Participant Review should normally preserve:

- Participant Review Identifier;
- author name;
- author role;
- organization;
- related Registry Identifier;
- related demonstration identifier;
- related case identifier;
- related finding identifier;
- submission date;
- publication date;
- publication permission;
- review text;
- attachments;
- supporting references;
- participant declaration;
- record status.

---

## 5.2 Participant Response

A Participant Response is an attributable response to a TA-14 finding, demonstration
artifact, evidence challenge, independent review, technical comment, factual
correction, or other governed record.

A Participant Response may:

- agree;
- disagree;
- qualify;
- clarify;
- contextualize;
- identify limitations;
- explain subsequent engineering decisions;
- preserve additional perspective;
- identify an unresolved dispute.

A Participant Response does not alter the underlying TA-14 finding merely because
the participant disagrees with it.

If a Participant Response identifies an objective factual error, that issue should
be routed through the Factual Correction pathway.

---

## 5.3 Independent Review

An Independent Review is submitted by a person or entity that was not responsible
for producing the underlying TA-14 finding or participant record being reviewed.

Independent Reviews may examine:

- evidence sufficiency;
- architecture boundaries;
- authority conditions;
- continuity;
- admissibility;
- binding;
- commit correspondence;
- execution behavior;
- runtime behavior;
- outcome evidence;
- provenance;
- chronology;
- methodology;
- implementation claims;
- findings;
- evidence limitations;
- demonstration design;
- route completeness.

Independent Reviews must remain attributable to their authors.

TA-14 may:

- acknowledge the review;
- publish the review;
- respond;
- accept conclusions;
- partially accept conclusions;
- dispute conclusions;
- decline to adopt conclusions;
- request additional evidence;
- open an evidence challenge;
- open a factual correction;
- open a new demonstration;
- preserve the review without institutional adoption.

The original Independent Review remains preserved regardless of TA-14 disposition.

---

## 5.4 Evidence Challenge

An Evidence Challenge identifies a specific evidentiary issue associated with a
registered claim, Governance Profile, demonstration, execution artifact, finding,
review, response, or related governed record.

A valid Evidence Challenge should identify:

1. the governed record being challenged;
2. the specific claim, artifact, evidence object, or finding at issue;
3. the basis for the challenge;
4. supporting evidence or source material;
5. challenger identity;
6. relevant affiliation;
7. potential conflicts;
8. requested disposition, if any.

Evidence Challenges should be evidence-based rather than rhetorical.

An Evidence Challenge may result in:

- NO_ACTION;
- REQUEST_ADDITIONAL_EVIDENCE;
- FACTUAL_CORRECTION;
- FINDING_REVIEW;
- NEW_DEMONSTRATION;
- PARTIAL_ACCEPTANCE;
- ACCEPTED_CHALLENGE;
- REJECTED_CHALLENGE;
- UNRESOLVED;
- ARCHIVED.

The underlying challenged record should remain visible.

---

## 5.5 Factual Correction Request

A Factual Correction Request concerns objective record accuracy.

Examples include:

- incorrect Registry Identifier;
- incorrect version;
- incorrect date;
- incorrect timestamp;
- incorrect artifact hash;
- incorrect author attribution;
- incorrect entity name;
- incorrect evidence description;
- incorrect source reference;
- transcription error;
- incorrectly associated attachment;
- broken institutional linkage.

A factual correction may result in a versioned correction to the affected record.

A factual correction must not become a pathway for negotiating an independent TA-14
determination.

Preferred correction model:

Original Record  
→ Correction Request  
→ TA-14 Review  
→ Corrected Version  
→ Revision Note  
→ Original Version Remains Preserved

---

## 5.6 Technical Comment

A Technical Comment provides substantive technical analysis that does not rise to the
level of a formal Independent Review or Evidence Challenge.

Technical Comments may address:

- implementation observations;
- standards analysis;
- architecture comparison;
- evidence questions;
- model behavior;
- methodological observations;
- technical boundaries;
- runtime conditions;
- interoperability;
- bounded criticism;
- future research;
- implementation questions;
- replication considerations.

Technical Comments remain attributable.

Technical Comments should remain linked to the governed object they address.

A Technical Comment does not become a TA-14 finding merely because it is published.

---

## 5.7 Replication Request

A Replication Request asks whether an existing governed demonstration, artifact,
finding, or execution route should be repeated under comparable conditions.

Replication Requests may ask to examine:

- reproducibility;
- replayability;
- implementation consistency;
- version correspondence;
- evidence continuity;
- outcome correspondence;
- repeat execution behavior;
- cross-environment behavior.

A Replication Request does not guarantee acceptance.

TA-14 retains responsibility for determining:

- scope;
- admissibility;
- evidence requirements;
- implementation freeze;
- resource requirements;
- demonstration boundaries;
- publication status.

---

## 5.8 Demonstration Request

A Demonstration Request asks TA-14 to consider a new bounded governance demonstration.

A request may concern:

- a registered governance architecture;
- runtime behavior;
- technical control;
- evidence mechanism;
- authority mechanism;
- execution route;
- refusal condition;
- escalation condition;
- consequence boundary;
- commit behavior;
- outcome evidence;
- version transition.

A Demonstration Request should identify:

- registered entity, where applicable;
- proposed version;
- bounded claim;
- proposed scenario;
- evidence available;
- execution surface;
- expected consequence;
- requested observation;
- relevant limitations.

Acceptance into the TA-14 demonstration process remains a separate institutional
decision.

---

## 5.9 External Publication Reference

External Publication References preserve independently authored material associated
with a governed TA-14 record.

Examples include:

- LinkedIn articles;
- Medium articles;
- Zenodo publications;
- academic papers;
- institutional reports;
- research papers;
- technical case studies;
- repository publications;
- independent analyses;
- conference papers;
- public responses.

External Publications remain under the responsibility and authorship of their authors.

TA-14 linking to an External Publication does not constitute adoption of its
conclusions.

The record should preserve:

- publication title;
- author;
- publisher or platform;
- publication date;
- URL;
- DOI, if available;
- related Registry Identifier;
- related case;
- relationship to the governed record;
- publication status.

# 6. Governed Messaging

The TA-14 AI Governance Exchange should provide a structured messaging pathway for
people who want to communicate with TA-14 about a specific governed record.

This pathway should not function as unrestricted social-media messaging.

A governed message should normally be attached to a relevant institutional object.

Examples include:

- Registry Entity;
- Governance Profile;
- Demonstration;
- Case;
- Finding;
- Execution Artifact;
- Evidence Object;
- Participant Review;
- Participant Response;
- Independent Review;
- Evidence Challenge;
- Factual Correction;
- Technical Comment;
- External Publication.

The message should preserve enough context that the communication remains
institutionally attributable.

# 9. Message Boundary

A message does not become evidence merely because it was received.

A message does not become a TA-14 finding merely because it was published.

A Technical Comment does not acquire institutional authority merely through
submission.

A review does not automatically become accepted institutional doctrine.

A participant statement does not rewrite the original registered baseline.

Each communication remains classified according to its own record type,
evidentiary status, attribution, and institutional disposition.

---

# 10. Public / Controlled / Private Boundary

Not every review, response, challenge, comment, or message should automatically
become public.

The system should support explicit visibility states.

---

## PUBLIC

The submitter authorizes publication and the submission satisfies TA-14 publication
requirements.

The public may inspect the record.

---

## CONTROLLED

The material is available to TA-14 reviewers, authorized participants, or other
designated parties but is not generally public.

---

## PRIVATE

The communication is preserved for institutional purposes but is not publicly
displayed.

---

## PUBLIC_METADATA_ONLY

Basic metadata may be visible while the substantive content remains controlled.

Possible public metadata includes:

- record type;
- submitter name;
- organization;
- submission date;
- related record;
- status.

---

# 11. Publication Permission

Publication permission should be explicit.

A submitter should be able to specify:

- PUBLICATION_ALLOWED;
- PUBLICATION_ALLOWED_WITH_ATTRIBUTION;
- PUBLICATION_METADATA_ONLY;
- CONTROLLED_USE_ONLY;
- PRIVATE;
- REQUIRES_FURTHER_APPROVAL.

TA-14 should not silently change the confidentiality or publication status of a
submission.

# 16. No Unrestricted Comment Feed

The TA-14 AI Governance Exchange should not become a social-media-style comment
system.

An unrestricted feed creates several problems:

- unattributable claims;
- chronology ambiguity;
- weak evidence boundaries;
- unsupported allegations;
- identity uncertainty;
- inability to distinguish review from commentary;
- inability to preserve dispute status;
- unclear publication rights;
- reduced institutional value.

Instead, the Exchange should provide structured record classes and governed intake.

The public should still be able to communicate.

But the communication should enter the record in a defined form.

---

# 17. Attribution Requirement

Anonymous substantive reviews should not normally become part of the governed public
record.

TA-14 may receive private confidential communications where appropriate.

Public reviews, challenges, responses, and technical comments should ordinarily
identify their author.

The public record should preserve:

Author  
→ Role  
→ Organization  
→ Date  
→ Record Addressed  
→ Submission Type  
→ Statement  
→ Evidence  
→ Disposition

---

# 18. Conflict and Affiliation Disclosure

Independent Reviews, Evidence Challenges, and other substantive public submissions
should support disclosure of relevant affiliations.

Examples include:

- employment;
- architecture ownership;
- consulting relationship;
- partnership;
- competitor relationship;
- financial interest;
- prior review relationship;
- research collaboration;
- institutional affiliation.

Disclosure does not automatically disqualify a submission.

It allows readers to evaluate the context of the contribution.

---

# 19. Evidence Attachment Rules

Where a submission includes supporting evidence, the Exchange should preserve:

- evidence filename;
- media type;
- submitted date;
- source;
- author or custodian;
- relationship to submission;
- visibility;
- hash;
- preservation state;
- description;
- version, where applicable.

Evidence submitted after an original demonstration should not be silently treated as
though it existed during the original event.

Later evidence may support:

- challenge;
- correction;
- subsequent finding;
- new demonstration;
- version update.

It must remain chronologically distinguishable.

---

# 20. Chronology Protection

Later submissions must never silently modify prior records.

Preferred chronology:

Original Record  
→ Participant Review  
→ Participant Response  
→ Independent Review  
→ Challenge  
→ TA-14 Disposition  
→ Correction, if warranted  
→ Subsequent Review  
→ Engineering Learning  
→ New Version  
→ New Baseline

If an earlier record contains a factual error, the correction should be preserved
through version history rather than silently replacing the historical state.

---

# 21. Version Lineage Rule

Where review, demonstration, or challenge produces subsequent engineering changes,
those changes should remain attributable to a later version or separately identified
implementation state.

Preferred lineage:

Version 1.0  
→ Registered Baseline  
→ Frozen Demonstration  
→ Evidence Admission  
→ Independent Finding  
→ Participant Review  
→ Engineering Learning  
→ Version 2 Development  
→ Version 2 Baseline  
→ Future Demonstration

The Version 1.0 record should remain intact.

Later improvement must not be presented as though it existed in the earlier version.

---

# 22. Participant Review Boundary

A Participant Review should normally be preserved exactly or substantially as
submitted.

TA-14 may add institutional metadata around the review.

TA-14 should not rewrite the participant's substantive conclusions into TA-14 voice.

A Participant Review may contain:

- favorable observations;
- criticism;
- disagreement;
- qualification;
- engineering lessons;
- process observations.

The presence of a Participant Review does not imply TA-14 adoption of the
participant's conclusions.

---

# 23. Independent Review Boundary

An Independent Review should remain distinguishable from:

- participant review;
- TA-14 finding;
- evidence challenge;
- technical comment.

An Independent Review should identify:

- author;
- review target;
- review scope;
- evidence reviewed;
- methodology;
- findings;
- limitations;
- conflicts or affiliations;
- publication permission.

---

# 24. Factual Review vs. Independent Finding

The Exchange should preserve the distinction between factual-record review and
substantive agreement.

A participant may confirm:

- identifiers;
- dates;
- timestamps;
- version numbers;
- artifact descriptions;
- evidence references;
- attribution.

That confirmation does not mean the participant approves the TA-14 finding.

A participant disagreement should be preserved as a separate response rather than
negotiated into the independent finding.

---
