# TA-14 Public Infrastructure Transition Plan v1

**Status:** Institutional transition proposal
**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Purpose:** Define a governed path by which TA-14 can move from founder-controlled commercial architecture toward broadly adoptable public-interest infrastructure without surrendering the stewardship capacity required to maintain, verify, improve, and expand it.

## 1. Governing objective

TA-14 should optimize for adoption, justified consequence, public usefulness, and durable stewardship rather than maximum extraction.

The intended destination is not simply "free software." The intended destination is infrastructure: architecture that can be implemented broadly while TA-14 preserves enough institutional authority and economics to maintain the specifications, protect identity, operate official evidence surfaces, perform examinations, support interoperability, educate implementers, and expand environmental-integrity infrastructure.

## 2. Core institutional principle

**Give away the architecture. Charge for stewardship and institutional acts.**

A party should not ultimately need to pay TA-14 merely to study or implement the public architecture. A party may pay TA-14 when TA-14 itself performs an institutional act or provides a maintained service, including examination, registration, certification, hosting, managed deployment, training, credentialing, implementation assistance, interoperability verification, or official network participation.

## 3. Five protected boundaries

### A. Public architecture
Candidate public components may include, after legal and technical review:
- architecture specifications;
- the canonical execution chain and supporting specifications;
- public schemas and evidence formats;
- reference implementations;
- conformance and replay test suites;
- interoperability protocols;
- selected Exchange components;
- Environmental Integrity Governance specifications;
- Atmospheric Integrity Record specifications;
- Personal Atmospheric Integrity Record specifications;
- public network-interface specifications.

No component becomes irrevocably public merely because it appears in this plan. Each release requires an explicit release decision and license review.

### B. Institutional identity and trademarks
TA-14 names, institutional marks, official certification marks, registry identity, credential marks, and representations of official TA-14 standing remain controlled. Public implementation of the architecture must not automatically imply TA-14 certification, endorsement, registration, examination, or institutional standing.

### C. Official institutional acts
TA-14 may preserve official authority over services it actually performs, including:
- official registry entries;
- independent examinations;
- certification/conformity determinations where offered;
- institutional credentials;
- official interoperability marks;
- hosted Exchange services;
- managed infrastructure;
- official Atmospheric Integrity Network participation or verification services.

### D. Stewardship economics
TA-14 retains the economic capacity reasonably required to operate, secure, maintain, improve, verify, defend, and expand the infrastructure. No permanent 5%, 10%, or other stewardship ceiling is adopted until real operating economics demonstrate what is sustainable.

### E. Public-benefit economics
Surplus economics should progressively expand public environmental integrity rather than default to maximum extraction. The public ledger should distinguish institutional operating requirements from environmental/public-benefit reinvestment.

## 4. Environmental Integrity Reinvestment architecture

### Standard commercial commitment
TA-14 designates **30% of qualifying commercial service receipts** to transparent environmental-integrity reinvestment under the published accounting definition in force for the program.

### Municipal / citywide deployments
A municipal, citywide, or similarly scaled deployment may dedicate **up to 50% of qualifying jurisdiction-specific TA-14 economics** to environmental-integrity reinvestment when the contract economics demonstrate that TA-14 can still sustainably operate and fulfill its obligations.

### Long-term destination
As adoption and scale reduce TA-14's proportional stewardship cost, the institution should evaluate whether the public-benefit percentage can rise beyond these initial commitments. The long-term objective is to retain what sustainable stewardship requires and direct additional surplus toward public infrastructure and environmental benefit.

## 5. Eligible environmental-benefit mission classes

Reinvestment must remain inside a governed environmental-benefit boundary. Eligible classes may include:

1. **Environmental Integrity Governance (EIG)** — deployment, education, evidence systems, implementation support, and qualifying public-interest applications.
2. **Atmospheric Integrity Records (AIR)** — building, facility, community, and network-scale atmospheric evidence infrastructure.
3. **Personal Atmospheric Integrity Records (PAIR / Personal AIR)** — qualifying personal atmospheric-integrity systems and access programs, including support for children, older adults, students, and underserved populations where appropriate.
4. **Critical animal environments** — zoos, conservation facilities, shelters, habitats, veterinary or other qualifying environments where atmospheric/environmental integrity materially supports the governed environment.
5. **Critical human environments** — schools, childcare environments, elder-care environments, and qualifying neonatal/incubation or similarly sensitive environments. Environmental monitoring or governance must not be represented as medical diagnosis, treatment, or proof of clinical outcome unless independently supported by appropriate clinical evidence and authority.
6. **Atmospheric Integrity Network infrastructure** — local, municipal, regional, national, and global interoperable infrastructure.
7. **Environmental-integrity research, education, and access** — qualifying research, schooling, training, deployment assistance, and public-interest access that advances the mission.

## 6. Direction rights

Where administratively and legally feasible, a customer or participating institution may express a preference for where the environmental allocation associated with its qualifying engagement is directed.

Permitted destination modes may include:
- own qualifying building/project;
- another qualifying property or organization;
- local community project;
- qualifying project elsewhere in the country;
- qualifying global project;
- Atlas-directed general pool.

Direction is not an unrestricted transfer right. Every destination remains subject to mission eligibility, legal review, evidence requirements, conflicts review, available funds, and program rules.

## 7. Petition-for-funds mechanism

When sufficient unallocated funds exist, eligible entities may petition for funding.

A petition should identify:
- applicant and responsible party;
- environmental problem or need;
- affected population/environment;
- requested amount;
- proposed intervention;
- budget and intended use;
- baseline evidence;
- expected measurable benefit;
- evidence that can be preserved during execution;
- post-intervention measurement plan;
- consent to required transparency;
- conflicts or related-party interests.

Possible bounded dispositions include APPROVED, HOLD, DENIED / OUTSIDE MISSION, INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, FUNDING UNAVAILABLE, or ESCALATED FOR REVIEW.

## 8. Atlas allocation stewardship

Atlas may serve as an AI allocation steward by researching eligible projects, comparing evidence, documenting recommendations, identifying conflicts or evidentiary gaps, and reviewing outcome records against the published charter.

Atlas does not independently own, bank, possess, or transfer money. Legal custody, fiduciary responsibility, contracting, regulatory compliance, and transfer authority remain with the responsible human or legal entity.

## 9. Radical transparency ledger

The public ledger should expose, subject to legitimate privacy, security, contractual, and legal restrictions:
- qualifying receipts;
- applicable reinvestment percentage;
- amount designated;
- allocated amount;
- unallocated balance;
- distributed amount;
- allocation identifier;
- recipient;
- jurisdiction;
- date;
- amount;
- customer-directed or Atlas-directed status;
- purpose;
- eligibility basis;
- selection rationale;
- conflicts review;
- proof of transfer;
- baseline environmental record;
- intervention/execution record;
- post-intervention evidence;
- outcome determination;
- limitations;
- returned or unused funds;
- follow-up status.

**A transfer receipt proves that money moved. It does not prove environmental impact.** Environmental-impact claims require evidence appropriate to the claim.

## 10. Municipal self-financing hypothesis

TA-14 should formally test the proposition that sufficiently scaled municipal adoption can finance a meaningful portion, or potentially all, of a jurisdiction's Atmospheric Integrity Network infrastructure from the environmental-reinvestment share of TA-14 economics.

This is currently a hypothesis, not an established economic fact.

Before making a self-financing claim, model at minimum:
- city population and building stock;
- deployment scope;
- TA-14 pricing and gross receipts;
- implementation and support cost;
- stewardship reserve;
- reinvestment percentage;
- sensor/node acquisition cost;
- installation cost;
- network/data infrastructure;
- annual operations;
- calibration/maintenance;
- replacement reserves;
- cybersecurity/data governance;
- expansion schedule;
- time to citywide coverage.

## 11. Open-infrastructure transition stages

### Stage 1 — Revenue and evidence
Sell current bounded services and deployments. Measure actual acquisition cost, delivery cost, support burden, infrastructure cost, margins, conversion, and environmental allocation capacity.

### Stage 2 — Public Infrastructure Constitution
Complete legal/IP inventory. Define public components, retained marks, institutional acts, contribution governance, certification boundaries, security governance, and economic safeguards.

### Stage 3 — Controlled open release
Release selected components under attorney-reviewed recognized licenses. Preserve provenance, versioning, conformance tests, security policy, contribution rules, and trademark boundaries.

### Stage 4 — Neutralized governance where beneficial
Evaluate whether mature specifications or network protocols should move into a neutral foundation, standards body, public-benefit entity, or multi-stakeholder governance structure while TA-14 remains a steward and service provider.

### Stage 5 — Infrastructure-scale economics
Use real operating evidence to determine the minimum sustainable institutional retention and progressively increase public-benefit reinvestment when justified.

## 12. Preconditions before irreversible licensing

No irreversible dedication, public-domain release, or broad relicensing should occur until:
- IP ownership and contributor rights are inventoried;
- third-party dependencies are reviewed;
- patents/potential patent rights are reviewed by qualified counsel;
- trademark strategy is established;
- certification/registry representations are legally separated from implementation rights;
- open-source licensing is reviewed by qualified counsel;
- tax/nonprofit/public-benefit implications are reviewed;
- contribution and security governance exist;
- a sustainable stewardship budget is modeled;
- the governing body explicitly authorizes the release.

## 13. Non-claims

This plan does not establish:
- tax-exempt status;
- charitable-deduction eligibility;
- a separate legal foundation;
- medical efficacy;
- clinical benefit;
- guaranteed environmental outcomes;
- guaranteed municipal self-financing;
- a present obligation to release proprietary rights;
- a fixed future stewardship percentage.

## 14. Institutional destination

TA-14 should seek a future in which the architecture can become broadly usable public-interest infrastructure; official institutional standing remains attributable and trustworthy; environmental reinvestment is transparent; implementers can build without artificial permission barriers; and TA-14 remains economically capable of maintaining the infrastructure it helped create.

**The destination is not: TA-14 became free.**

**The destination is: TA-14 became infrastructure.**
