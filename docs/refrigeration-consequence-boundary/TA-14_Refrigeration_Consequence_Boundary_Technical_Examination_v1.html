Refrigeration Cybersecurity, Physical Drift, and Admissible Evidence

1. Executive summary

This examination asks whether an unauthorized change to refrigeration equipment can be prevented when it passes through TA-14's Admissible Execution Architecture (AEA), detected by independent observation when it bypasses AEA, and preserved as an inspectable incident record. Central principle: an attacker may bypass the governance boundary without automatically escaping independent observation and evidence preservation. That is a conditional capability, not a guarantee.

Eight adversarial scenarios were run. Across them, 27 examined claims passed, 3 failed and 0 were not demonstrated:

  • Prevention held for commands submitted through AEA (S1, S7) and failed for commands that bypassed it (S2, S3). AEA cannot refuse a command it never sees.
  • Detection by independent sensors succeeded in S2–S5 (S2 after 10 min, S3 after 35 min, S4 after 35 min, S5 after 35 min), including against a controller that falsified its reports (S4) and a suppressed alarm (S5). It failed in S6, when the independent temperature evidence was lost; the gap was disclosed, and the corrective action was held for lack of admissible evidence.
  • Evidence preservation: all eight hash-chained records verify; five tamper types are detected; no authorization was fabricated for bypassed commands; no cause was attributed.
  • Emergency pathway (extension E1, section 9A): with independent evidence lost, a pre-authorized protective measure that only re-asserts a sealed-baseline value was allowed; consequential commands are never available on that path; ordinary governance still held. Detection and outcome verification remained NOT DEMONSTRATED. 22 adversarial tests show the pathway cannot be used to bypass ordinary governance. S6 is preserved as a failure case.
  • Recovery (S8): a routine command was held during the open incident, a corrective authority revoked between commit and execution was refused at the execution boundary, and a fresh authority restored the baseline, verified afterwards by independent observation.

2. Origin of the challenge

This is an independent TA-14 technical examination. It examines a question that arises wherever refrigeration equipment is networked: what happens when an unauthorized actor changes equipment behaviour outside its intended governance boundary? No external party has reviewed, participated in, or endorsed this examination or its conclusions.

4. Threat model and trust boundaries

Modelled attacker capabilities: submitting commands through the governed route without authority; replaying a genuine expired authority; writing directly to the controller (defrost, setpoint); making the controller report false temperatures; suppressing the controller alarm. Independently modelled failure: loss or sticking of the independent temperature probes.

Not modelled: compromise of the independent probes, their loggers or the recorder; theft of a current, unexpired authority together with the holder's identity; physical tampering; network-level attacks; real controller protocols.

Trust boundaries: (1) the AEA gate governs only commands that pass through it; (2) the controller's self-reports are recorded but treated as untrusted (independentOfController: false); (3) the EIN's independence from the controller is an assumption of the simulation, which real sites must establish; (4) the simulation's ground truth exists only for examination and is never supplied to the EIN, EIR or gate.

8. EIN/EIR evidence preservation

Each entry records sequence, recorder time, observation time, source (EIN, AEA_GATE, CONTROLLER_REPORTED, HUMAN, RECORDER), independence from the controller, payload, the previous entry's hash and its own SHA-256 over canonical JSON. The sealed baseline is stored separately and anchors the chain (genesis = seal). Verification recomputes every hash and link, sequence continuity, monotonic recorder time and the chain head, in Node or in a browser via WebCrypto (same code).

Limitations: entries are not signed and carry no trusted third-party time-stamp. A valid chain shows the export was not altered; it does not show the sensors were right, and an existing record is not automatically admissible.

9A. Emergency-response pathway (extension)

S6 is preserved unchanged as a documented failure: with independent evidence lost, ordinary governance held the corrective action and the product warmed. The GCEA engine is not modified to force an ALLOW. Instead, a separately authorized pathway is defined with three tiers that are never interchangeable.

TierPermittedRequired
E0 Emergency notificationNotify the responder and facility manager. No equipment effect.An evidence-loss trigger; always recorded.
E1 Protective measureRESTORE_SEALED_SETPOINT: Set the controller setpoint to the value in the sealed baseline.
TERMINATE_ACTIVE_DEFROST: End any defrost in progress (cooling resumes under the thermostat).
RE_ENABLE_CONTROLLER_ALARM: Re-enable the controller's own high-temperature alarm.
Each takes no parameters and only re-asserts a sealed-baseline value.		Protocol RCB-EP-1.0 bound to the sealed baseline; a pre-issued GCEA authority scoped to route emergency-protective-route and the specific measure; emergency-responder standing; independent evidence lost for ≥ 15 min, computed from the EIN record (never caller-supplied); one use per measure per incident; revalidation at execution, including the condition; ordinary reauthorization required.
E2 Consequential commandDefrost, any other setpoint, disabling alarms or reporting.Not available on the emergency path; ordinary governance only.

Evidence appropriate to the action. E1's evidence is the recorded, continuous EIN determination that state cannot be established, combined with the structural guarantee that the measure can only restore sealed values. GCEA receives that evidence standing honestly. When independent evidence is available, the emergency route is refused (DENY), so it cannot substitute for ordinary governance. If evidence returns before execution, execution is refused.

Adversarial tests (22, all passing): evidence available → DENY; loss not yet sustained → HOLD; actor without responder standing (even with a stolen grant) → DENY; revoked standing → DENY; consequential commands (defrost, setpoint +2 °C, disabling the alarm) → DENY; consequential actions disguised as protective measures (four variants) → DENY; smuggled parameters → DENY; no authority → refused; an ordinary grant on the emergency route → DENY (scope mismatch); one measure's grant for another → DENY; an emergency grant on the ordinary route → DENY; expired grant → DENY; revocation between commit and execution → DENY; evidence returning before execution → DENY; reuse in the same incident → DENY; protocol bound to another baseline → DENY; a caller-supplied condition is ignored.

11. Known limitations and untested conditions

  • No physical-system validation: the thermal model is illustrative, with unmeasured parameters.
  • Detection depends on independent sensors that exist, are installed correctly and are genuinely independent of the compromised path. Attacks on the sensors, loggers or recorder were not modelled.
  • S6: with independent evidence lost, the corrective action was held ("no admissible evidence, no admissible execution") while the controller's untrusted alarm fired, and the simulated product kept warming. An emergency procedure for acting without admissible evidence is needed in practice and is not modelled.
  • The actor-standing and parameter-bounds checks are simulation additions around GCEA, not production components. EIN and EIR here are simulations, not the EIE implementation.
  • Records are hash-chained but unsigned, with no trusted time-stamp. Notifications and human actions are simulated.
  • Thresholds are configured for the simulation; food-safety limits and product disposition are outside TA-14's determination.
  • Narration is browser speech synthesis. Its controls were exercised in a real (headless) browser, but audible playback was not verified.
  • The examination is owner-gated (404 for everyone else; fails closed). Access by the real signed-in owner was not exercised locally, because no live Supabase session was available; the gate was verified closed in every other configuration.
  • Not examined: subtle drift (for example −22 → −19 °C), slow sensor drift, multiple simultaneous faults, theft of a current authority, real network paths and protocols.
