# TA-14 Global Admissible Execution Exchange

> **No admissible evidence. No admissible execution.**

**TA-14** is a publication-backed, evidence-first governance architecture for determining whether consequence-bearing execution has standing **before consequence is allowed to bind to reality**.

The **TA-14 Global Admissible Execution Exchange** is the public operating environment for that architecture: a multi-domain workspace for governance registration, route construction, governed records, execution artifacts, interoperability demonstrations, independent examination, education, environmental integrity governance, and public institutional evidence.

TA-14 is not merely a policy library, model wrapper, logging dashboard, runtime guardrail, compliance checklist, refusal layer, receipt engine, or post-event audit trail.

It asks a harder question:

> **Can an independent examiner reconstruct why consequence was allowed to bind to reality—and determine whether that standing survived until execution?**

# Canonical TA-14 chain

```text
REALITY
   ↓
RECORD
   ↓
CONTINUITY
   ↓
ADMISSIBILITY
   ↓
BINDING
   ↓
COMMIT
   ↓
EXECUTION
   ↓
OUTCOME
   ↘
    NEW / REVALIDATED REALITY
             ↓
         NEXT CHAIN
```

The eight public anchors are distinct evidentiary burdens, not decorative workflow labels.

## 1. Reality

What governed state actually exists?

Reality is not whatever a dashboard, model, sensor, policy engine, or previous record says exists. It is the state against which a consequential decision must ultimately stand.

The architecture begins outside the record because an internally consistent record cannot prove its own completeness.

## 2. Record

What evidence represents that reality?

A record must be attributable, bounded, reviewable, and fit for the consequential question.

TA-14 distinguishes **tamper evidence** from **omission detectability**. A cryptographically intact record can still omit a material event. A strong evidence regime must make material absence detectable rather than allowing silence to masquerade as an uneventful reality.

## 3. Continuity

Did the governed relationship survive change?

Continuity preserves the relationship among reality, evidence, identity, chronology, custody, authority, scope, constraints, dependencies, and consequential state.

A valid state at `T0` does not automatically establish valid standing at `T1`.

Continuity asks what changed, when it changed, under whose authority, whether the evidence still describes the same governed reality, and whether revalidation is required.

## 4. Admissibility

Does the present evidence have sufficient standing for the proposed consequence?

Evidence may exist and still be inadmissible. Authority may have existed and no longer be valid. A historically valid decision may require present revalidation.

Admissibility prevents existence, confidence, approval, telemetry, or prior validity from being mistaken for present consequential standing.

## 5. Binding

What consequence is permitted to attach, to whom or what, within what scope, and under what authority?

Binding turns an admissible determination into an explicit consequential boundary.

Governance cannot end at recommendation or approval if the production executor remains free to create a different consequence.

## 6. Commit

What bounded intent is actually being committed for execution?

Commit is deliberately separate from execution.

A recommendation is not execution. A model output is not execution. An approval is not execution. Commit identifies the attributable intent that is actually being authorized to cross the execution boundary.

## 7. Execution

What consequential act actually occurred?

Execution is governed only when the real production path remains bound to the admissible determination and committed boundary.

A governance mechanism can compute the correct answer and still fail to govern if an equivalent consequential path can bypass it.

## 8. Outcome

What actually resulted?

Outcome reconnects consequence to evidence. It records whether execution occurred as committed, what effect followed, and what new reality now exists.

An outcome closes a bounded chain. It does **not** create permanent authority for future execution.

# Primary determinations

| Determination | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ALLOW** | Sufficient present standing exists to proceed within the governed boundary. |
| **HOLD** | Execution pauses because a required evidentiary, continuity, authority, transition, or scope condition is unresolved. |
| **DENY** | The route does not have standing to proceed. |
| **ESCALATE** | Another authority, review, evidence package, or governed intervention is required before consequence may proceed. |

These are not ordinary workflow statuses. Their legitimacy depends on the admissibility of the route that produced them.

A strong governance system should prove not only **why it acted**, but also **why it refused to act when the chain failed**.

TA-14 is built around a shift from:

```text
GOVERNANCE AS ASSERTION
        ↓
GOVERNANCE AS INSPECTABLE EVIDENCE
        ↓
INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION
        ↓
COUNTERFACTUAL CHALLENGE
        ↓
EXECUTOR BINDING
        ↓
OUTCOME ACCOUNTABILITY
```

Instead of “we have governance,” the stronger standard is:

> **Here is the bounded record. Inspect the evidence. Inspect the authority. Inspect the transitions. Reconstruct the determination. Verify what bound, what committed, what executed, and what outcome followed.**

# The hardest boundary: Reality → Record

Everything after a record exists can potentially be inspected through artifacts. The first arrow is harder.

A record cannot prove its own completeness merely because it is authentic.

TA-14 therefore treats **omission detectability** as distinct from alteration detection.

A defensible capture regime may require defined capture obligations, expected event sequences, expected state transitions, temporal continuity, sequence continuity, gap detection, independently anchored observations, exception records, and unresolved-absence states.

> **A material absence must not silently inherit standing.**

If an expected event or state transition has no corresponding record, the absence itself becomes a continuity and admissibility question.

---

# Present standing: T0 → T1

A governance record can be complete and still be stale.

A determination that was admissible at `T0` may cease to be admissible before the next consequential handoff at `T1`.

Material changes may include revoked or expired authority, changed identity, changed scope, changed constraints, changed dependencies, changed environmental state, removed prerequisites, superseding evidence, new risk, changed consequence, or changed jurisdiction/policy basis.

The relevant question is not merely “was execution authorized?”

> **Was it still admissible when consequence attached?**

This is why TA-14 separates **Continuity** from **Admissibility**.

---

# Independent reconstruction, counterfactual replay, and executor binding

Independent reconstruction asks whether an examiner can reproduce the consequential determination from the evidence without trusting the originating system's assertion.

A reconstruction should answer:

1. What was the governing reality?
2. What evidence represented it?
3. What changed?
4. What authority and scope applied?
5. What determination was justified?
6. What consequence was allowed to bind?
7. What was committed?
8. What executed?
9. What outcome followed?

Counterfactual replay then changes a material fact and recomputes standing.

```text
CHANGE MATERIAL STATE
        ↓
RECOMPUTE CONTINUITY / ADMISSIBILITY
        ↓
RE-EVALUATE BINDING / COMMIT
        ↓
VERIFY THE DETERMINATION CHANGES WHEN IT SHOULD
```

Revoked authority should not reproduce `ALLOW`. Missing evidence should not inherit a prior decision. Changed identity or scope should trigger reassessment. Expired prerequisites and changed constraints should alter standing appropriately.

Replay is still insufficient if production execution can bypass the governed route.

The distinction between **proving a determination** and **proving control over consequence** is fundamental.

A stronger route-closure test asks:

> **Can anything materially equivalent still happen outside the admissibility chain?**

---

# Authority, signatures, and approval gates

Signatures and approval gates can provide important evidence of attribution and authorization. They are not automatically proof of continuing standing.

A signature may prove **who approved**. An approval may prove **what was authorized**. Neither alone proves that the authority remained admissible when execution committed.

That is why TA-14 separates:

```text
ADMISSIBILITY → BINDING → COMMIT → EXECUTION
```

The consequential question is whether authority remained valid against the governing reality when consequence attached.

# Evidence boundaries and non-claim discipline

The strength of a finding must remain proportional to the strength of the evidence.

Unless evidence and engagement scope support it, TA-14 materials should not be interpreted as certification, legal advice/opinion, regulatory approval, security audit, compliance certification, production validation, third-party endorsement, or proof that unobserved private capabilities do or do not exist.

Public-material review supports public-material findings. Supplied operational evidence may support stronger findings. Route-complete evidence is required for route-complete conclusions.

# Repository change governance

A repository implementing admissible execution should apply continuity discipline to its own changes.

```text
CHANGE INTENT
    ↓
CURRENT MAIN REALITY
    ↓
BOUNDED CHANGE
    ↓
INSPECTABLE DIFF
    ↓
VERIFICATION
    ↓
MERGE DETERMINATION
    ↓
MAIN
    ↓
PRODUCTION
    ↓
DEPLOYMENT EVIDENCE
```

`main` is the authoritative integration branch. Consequential work should normally begin from current `main`. Superseded PRs should be closed. Stale branches should not be merged merely because their original idea was once valid. Unique work should be re-established against current reality and verified again.

> **Historical validity does not automatically create present standing.**

Branch protection, deletion/force-push prevention, appropriate status checks, and bounded pull-request review are desirable institutional continuity controls.

# How to examine TA-14

A serious review should ask:

1. Is the governing reality identifiable?
2. Is the record sufficient and attributable?
3. Can material omission be detected?
4. Is chronology preserved?
5. Is continuity preserved across state transition?
6. Is the evidence admissible for the proposed consequence?
7. Is authority valid and in scope?
8. Is the binding boundary explicit?
9. Is commit attributable and bounded?
10. Is the actual executor constrained by that boundary?
11. Can materially equivalent bypass routes exist?
12. Can the transaction be independently reconstructed?
13. Does counterfactual replay change the determination when it should?
14. Is refusal / HOLD / DENY / ESCALATE behavior evidenced?
15. Does outcome proof reconnect to the consequence basis?
16. Is the resulting new reality governed before the next chain begins?

If another architecture can answer these questions more completely, it should survive comparison.

**The test is evidence, not branding.**
