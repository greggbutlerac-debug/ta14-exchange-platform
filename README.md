# TA-14 Global Admissible Execution Exchange

> **No admissible evidence. No admissible execution.**

The **TA-14 Global Admissible Execution Exchange** is the public operating environment for TA-14: a publication-backed, evidence-first governance architecture for determining whether consequence-bearing execution has standing **before consequence is allowed to bind to reality**.

TA-14 is not merely a policy library, a model wrapper, a logging dashboard, a runtime guardrail, a compliance checklist, a refusal layer, a receipt engine, or a post-event audit trail.

It asks a harder question:

> **Can an independent examiner reconstruct why consequence was allowed to bind to reality—and determine whether that standing survived until execution?**

The Exchange exists to make that question operational.

# The canonical TA-14 chain

```text
REALITY
   ↓
RECORD
   ↓
CONTINUITY
   ↓
ADMISSIBILITY
   ↓
BINDING
   ↓
COMMIT
   ↓
EXECUTION
   ↓
OUTCOME
   ↘
    NEW / REVALIDATED REALITY
             ↓
         NEXT CHAIN
```

The eight public anchors are not decorative workflow labels. Each represents a distinct evidentiary burden.

## 1. Reality

What governed state actually exists?

Reality is not whatever a dashboard, model, sensor, policy engine, or previous record says exists. It is the state against which a consequential decision must ultimately stand.

The architecture begins outside the record because an internally consistent record cannot prove its own completeness.

## 2. Record

What evidence represents that reality?

A record must be attributable, bounded, reviewable, and fit for the consequential question.

TA-14 distinguishes **tamper evidence** from **omission detectability**. A cryptographically intact record can still omit a material event. A strong evidence regime must make material absence detectable rather than allowing silence to masquerade as an uneventful reality.

## 3. Continuity

Did the governed relationship survive change?

Continuity preserves the relationship among reality, evidence, identity, chronology, custody, authority, scope, constraints, dependencies, and consequential state.

A valid state at `T0` does not automatically establish valid standing at `T1`.

Continuity asks what changed, when it changed, under whose authority, whether the evidence still describes the same governed reality, and whether revalidation is required.

## 4. Admissibility

Does the present evidence have sufficient standing for the proposed consequence?

Evidence may exist and still be inadmissible. Authority may have existed and no longer be valid. A historically valid decision may require present revalidation.

Admissibility prevents existence, confidence, approval, telemetry, or prior validity from being mistaken for present consequential standing.

## 5. Binding

What consequence is permitted to attach, to whom or what, within what scope, and under what authority?

Binding turns an admissible determination into an explicit consequential boundary.

Governance cannot end at recommendation or approval if the production executor remains free to create a different consequence.

## 6. Commit

What bounded intent is actually being committed for execution?

Commit is deliberately separate from execution.

A recommendation is not execution. A model output is not execution. An approval is not execution. Commit identifies the attributable intent that is actually being authorized to cross the execution boundary.

## 7. Execution

What consequential act actually occurred?

Execution is governed only when the real production path remains bound to the admissible determination and committed boundary.

A governance mechanism can compute the correct answer and still fail to govern if an equivalent consequential path can bypass it.

## 8. Outcome

What actually resulted?

Outcome reconnects consequence to evidence.

It records whether execution occurred as committed, what effect followed, whether the intended consequence was achieved, and what new reality now exists.

An outcome closes a bounded chain. It does **not** create permanent authority for future execution.

# Primary determinations

TA-14 uses four primary governance determinations:

| Determination | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ALLOW** | Sufficient present standing exists to proceed within the governed boundary. |
| **HOLD** | Execution must pause because a required evidentiary, continuity, authority, transition, or scope condition is unresolved. |
| **DENY** | The route does not have standing to proceed. |
| **ESCALATE** | Another authority, review, evidence package, or governed intervention is required before consequence may proceed. |

These are not ordinary workflow statuses. Their legitimacy depends on the route that produced them.

A strong governance system must be able to prove not only **why it acted**, but also **why it refused to act when the chain failed**.

# The hardest boundary: Reality → Record

Everything after a record exists can potentially be inspected through artifacts. The first arrow is harder.

A record cannot prove its own completeness merely because it is authentic.

TA-14 therefore treats **omission detectability** as distinct from alteration detection.

A defensible capture regime may require:

- defined capture obligations;
- expected event sequences;
- expected state transitions;
- temporal continuity;
- sequence continuity;
- gap detection;
- independently anchored observations;
- exception records;
- unresolved-absence states.

> **A material absence must not silently inherit standing.**

If an expected event or state transition has no corresponding record, the absence itself becomes a continuity and admissibility question.

# Present standing: the T0 → T1 problem

A governance record can be complete and still be stale.

A determination that was admissible at `T0` may cease to be admissible before the next consequential handoff at `T1`.

Material changes may include:

- revoked or expired authority;
- changed identity;
- changed scope;
- changed constraints;
- changed dependencies;
- changed environmental state;
- removed prerequisites;
- superseding evidence;
- new risk;
- changed consequence;
- changed jurisdiction or policy basis.

The relevant question is not merely:

> “Was execution authorized?”

It is:

> **Was it still admissible when consequence attached?**

This is why TA-14 separates **Continuity** from **Admissibility**.

# Independent reconstruction

Governance becomes stronger when an independent examiner can reconstruct the consequential transaction from the evidence and reach the same determination without trusting the originating system's assertion.

A reconstruction should be able to answer:

1. What was the governing reality?
2. What evidence represented it?
3. What changed?
4. What authority applied?
5. What scope applied?
6. What determination was justified?
7. What consequence was allowed to bind?
8. What was committed?
9. What executed?
10. What outcome followed?

If those questions cannot be answered, governance may have been asserted without being independently evidenced.

---

# Counterfactual replay

Independent reconstruction asks whether the historical determination can be reproduced.

Counterfactual replay asks whether the governance mechanism responds correctly when a material fact is changed.

```text
CHANGE MATERIAL STATE
        ↓
RECOMPUTE CONTINUITY / ADMISSIBILITY
        ↓
RE-EVALUATE BINDING / COMMIT
        ↓
VERIFY THE DETERMINATION CHANGES WHEN IT SHOULD
```

Examples:

- revoked authority should not reproduce `ALLOW`;
- missing evidence should not silently inherit a prior determination;
- changed identity should trigger reassessment;
- changed scope should trigger reassessment;
- an expired prerequisite should alter standing;
- a superseded authority should not remain binding;
- changed constraints should produce the appropriate `HOLD`, `DENY`, or `ESCALATE`.

Replay alone is still insufficient if production execution can bypass the governed route.

---

# Executor binding and route closure

The distinction between **proving a determination** and **proving control over consequence** is fundamental.

A governance system does not control execution merely because it can demonstrate that its own decision engine would have returned the correct answer.

For consequence to be governed, the actual production path must be bound to the governing determination.

A stronger route-closure test asks:

> **Can anything materially equivalent still happen outside the admissibility chain?**

This is where governance moves from advisory correctness to consequential control.

---

# Authority, signatures, and approval gates

Signatures and approval gates can provide important evidence of attribution and authorization.

They are not automatically proof of continuing standing.

A signature may prove **who approved**.  
An approval may prove **what was authorized**.

Neither alone proves that the authority remained admissible when execution committed.

That is why the architecture separates:

```text
ADMISSIBILITY → BINDING → COMMIT → EXECUTION
```

The consequential question is whether authority remained valid **against the governing reality at the moment consequence attached**.

# DOI and publication interpretation

The Zenodo corpus should be interpreted carefully.

A DOI or Zenodo record preserves:

- authorship;
- title;
- public chronology;
- version identity;
- referenceability;
- publication lineage.

A DOI does **not** by itself establish:

- certification;
- legal opinion;
- regulatory approval;
- security audit;
- production validation;
- compliance determination;
- endorsement;
- implementation conformance.

The route still carries the burden of proof.

The **40 Zenodo records** should also not be treated as 40 interchangeable “core DOIs.” Some are foundational architecture monographs, some are extended architecture publications, some are standards, some are software/reference implementations, and some are earlier public doctrine records.

That distinction is intentional.

# Recursive reality governance

The chain does not end in a governance vacuum.

Execution produces outcome. Outcome changes reality. That new reality must itself become governed before a later consequence relies upon it.

```text
REALITY₀
  ↓
RECORD
  ↓
CONTINUITY
  ↓
ADMISSIBILITY
  ↓
BINDING
  ↓
COMMIT
  ↓
EXECUTION
  ↓
OUTCOME
  ↓
NEW REALITY
  ↓
REVALIDATION / NEW RECORD
  ↓
NEXT ADMISSIBILITY CHAIN
```

TA-14 Admissible Reality Architecture formalizes this recursive boundary:

Core principle:

> **No admissible reality. No admissible execution. No governed new reality. No admissible next chain.**

# Evidence boundaries and non-claim discipline

TA-14 distinguishes among levels of evidence.

Public materials may support a public-material finding. Supplied operational artifacts may support a stronger operational-evidence finding. Route-complete evidence may support stronger route conclusions.

Those boundaries must not be collapsed.

Unless the evidence and engagement scope support it, TA-14 materials should not be interpreted as:

- certification;
- legal advice or legal opinion;
- regulatory approval;
- security audit;
- compliance certification;
- production validation;
- endorsement of a third-party system;
- proof that unobserved private capabilities do or do not exist.

The strength of a finding must remain proportional to the strength of the evidence.

# Repository change governance

A repository implementing admissible execution should apply continuity discipline to its own changes.

```text
CHANGE INTENT
    ↓
CURRENT MAIN REALITY
    ↓
BOUNDED BRANCH / CHANGE
    ↓
INSPECTABLE DIFF
    ↓
VERIFICATION
    ↓
MERGE DETERMINATION
    ↓
MAIN
    ↓
PRODUCTION DEPLOYMENT
    ↓
DEPLOYMENT EVIDENCE
```

Repository principles:

- `main` is the authoritative integration branch.
- Consequential work should normally begin from current `main`.
- Pull requests should contain bounded, understandable changes.
- Superseded pull requests should be closed rather than left to imply unresolved institutional work.
- A stale branch should not be merged merely because its original idea was valid.
- Unique work from an old branch should be re-established against current reality and verified again.
- Historical validation does not automatically create present validation standing.
- A successful deployment proves execution occurred; it does not by itself prove governance was complete.
- Force-push and deletion protection for the authoritative branch are desirable continuity controls.
- Required status checks and pull-request controls should be introduced in a way that protects continuity without preventing legitimate institutional recovery.

This is the repository equivalent of the TA-14 continuity rule:

> **Historical validity does not automatically create present standing.**

# How to examine TA-14

TA-14 is designed to be challenged.

A serious review should not begin with:

> “Do I agree with TA-14?”

It should begin with questions such as:

1. Is the governing reality identifiable?
2. Is the record sufficient and attributable?
3. Can material omission be detected?
4. Is chronology preserved?
5. Is continuity preserved across state transition?
6. Can changed reality still reach the route before consequence becomes effectively irreversible?
7. Is the evidence admissible for the proposed consequence?
8. Is authority valid and in scope?
9. Is the binding boundary explicit?
10. Is commit attributable and bounded?
11. Is the actual executor constrained by that boundary?
12. Can materially equivalent bypass routes exist?
13. Can the transaction be independently reconstructed?
14. Does counterfactual replay change the determination when it should?
15. Is refusal / HOLD / DENY / ESCALATE behavior evidenced?
16. Does outcome proof reconnect to the consequence basis?
17. Is the resulting new reality governed before the next chain begins?
18. Is future memory/reliance supported by admissible outcome?
19. Can claimed non-occurrence or prevented consequence be proven without inventing history?
20. Can external egress, human intervention, and federated authority exchange be reconstructed within the same evidentiary discipline?

If another architecture can answer these questions more completely, it should survive comparison.

The test is evidence, not branding.
