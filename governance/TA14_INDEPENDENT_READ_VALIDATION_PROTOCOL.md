# TA14 Independent-Read Validation Protocol

**Instrument ID:** TA14-IRVP-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** PUBLIC GOVERNANCE PROTOCOL  
**Effective Date:** August 7, 2026  
**Issued By:** TA14 Authority

---

## 1. Purpose

The TA14 Independent-Read Validation Protocol establishes a repeatable method for determining whether a material TA14 Authority governance instrument can be understood from the published record itself without relying on private conversations, institutional memory, drafting history, or unstated intent.

The governing question is:

> **Can an outside reader determine what this instrument means without having participated in its creation?**

A governance instrument that requires private explanation to produce the intended interpretation is not yet sufficiently self-contained.

---

## 2. Governing Principle

TA14 Authority distinguishes between:

**Informed understanding**

and

**independent readability.**

A person involved in creating, discussing, or negotiating an instrument may understand its intended meaning because that person possesses context not contained in the document.

That understanding does not establish that an independent reader would reach the same conclusion.

Therefore:

> **The informed reader's ability to understand an instrument is not evidence that an uninformed third party can understand it.**

---

## 3. Scope

This protocol should be applied to material institutional instruments including:

- registration terms;
- evidence terms;
- artifact terms;
- review protocols;
- finding schemas;
- participant-response rules;
- withdrawal rules;
- publication rules;
- confidentiality rules;
- evidence-admission standards;
- registry governance instruments;
- certification-readiness instruments;
- material standards;
- institutional claim-boundary instruments;
- other documents that create or materially affect participant rights, obligations, interpretations, or institutional findings.

---

# PART I — REVIEW ROLES

## 4. Informed Reviewer

The Informed Reviewer understands the intended institutional operation of the instrument.

The reviewer may know:

- why the instrument was created;
- the underlying TA14 architecture;
- drafting history;
- prior conversations;
- intended outcomes;
- institutional terminology;
- implementation context.

The Informed Reviewer answers:

> **Does the instrument accurately express the rule TA14 Authority intends to establish?**

---

## 5. Independent Reader

The Independent Reader should not depend on private drafting context to interpret the instrument.

Where practicable, the Independent Reader should not have participated materially in:

- drafting the instrument;
- negotiating its terms;
- establishing its principal rules;
- conversations that explain ambiguities not contained in the instrument.

The Independent Reader answers:

> **What does this instrument mean based only on what the instrument actually says?**

---

## 6. Independence of the Read

The purpose of the Independent Reader is not to establish absolute personal independence.

The purpose is to remove dependence on **unstated drafting context**.

An Independent Reader may know what TA14 Authority is.

An Independent Reader may understand governance.

An Independent Reader may possess relevant professional expertise.

The reader should simply not require private knowledge of what the drafters intended.

---

# PART II — REVIEW SEQUENCE

## 7. Stage One — Draft Freeze

Before independent-read testing begins, the candidate instrument should receive a temporary review identifier and version.

Example:

**TA14-RET-001 — Candidate Version 1.0**

The tested body should then be frozen for that review cycle.

If material changes occur, the changed version must be distinguishable from the version previously reviewed.

---

## 8. Stage Two — Informed Review

The Informed Reviewer evaluates whether the instrument accurately represents intended institutional operation.

The reviewer should identify:

- incorrect rules;
- omitted boundaries;
- unintended permissions;
- unintended restrictions;
- undefined material terms;
- contradictions;
- missing non-claims;
- incorrect process sequences;
- unclear authority boundaries.

The result is recorded as:

**INFORMED REVIEW COMPLETE**

or

**INFORMED REVIEW REVISION REQUIRED**

---

## 9. Stage Three — Independent-Read Review

The Independent Reader receives the candidate instrument without relying on private explanatory material.

The reader should answer a standardized question set.

The reader should be encouraged to identify:

- what the instrument clearly establishes;
- what it clearly prohibits;
- what remains ambiguous;
- what appears implied rather than stated;
- what appears missing;
- where multiple reasonable interpretations exist.

The objective is not to test memory.

The objective is to test the instrument.

---

# PART III — RESPONSE STATES

## 10. Independent-Read Response States

For each material question, the Independent Reader should select one of the following:

### CLEAR

The instrument directly supports a reasonably definite answer.

### CLEAR WITH QUALIFICATION

The answer is available, but a material qualification is necessary.

### AMBIGUOUS

Two or more materially different reasonable interpretations remain possible.

### IMPLIED ONLY

The reader can infer a likely answer, but the instrument does not state it adequately.

### NOT STATED

The instrument does not provide the answer.

### INTERNALLY CONFLICTED

Different parts of the instrument appear to support materially inconsistent answers.

---

## 11. No Favorable Interpretation Default

An ambiguous or missing provision must not automatically receive the interpretation most favorable to TA14 Authority.

Likewise, it must not automatically receive the interpretation most favorable to a registrant.

The defect is the ambiguity itself.

---

# PART IV — STANDARD QUESTION SET

## 12. Registration Questions

For registration instruments, the Independent Reader should be able to answer:

1. What does registration establish?
2. What does registration explicitly not establish?
3. Does registration mean certification?
4. Does registration mean endorsement?
5. Does registration establish regulatory compliance?
6. Does registration establish technical correctness?
7. What party is responsible for the registered declaration?
8. Can the registration later be superseded?
9. Can the registration be withdrawn?
10. What remains after withdrawal?

---

## 13. Evidence Questions

The Independent Reader should be able to answer:

1. What evidence may be submitted?
2. Must source code be submitted?
3. Can a closed implementation remain closed?
4. Who owns submitted evidence?
5. What rights does TA14 receive?
6. What evidence may be published?
7. What happens to private evidence?
8. What happens when evidence was never preserved?
9. What happens when evidence is missing?
10. Does a hash establish truth?
11. Does preservation establish independence?
12. How is registrant-produced evidence distinguished from independent evidence?

---

## 14. Finding Questions

The Independent Reader should be able to answer:

1. What does a finding apply to?
2. Does a finding extend beyond the reviewed version?
3. Can a limitation be separated from the finding it qualifies?
4. Can TA14 issue a mixed or qualified finding?
5. Does participation require a PASS?
6. Can a registrant disagree?
7. Does disagreement rewrite the original finding?
8. Can a factual error be corrected?
9. Is the correction visible?
10. Can TA14 silently modify an earlier finding?

---

## 15. Independence Questions

The Independent Reader should be able to answer:

1. Does TA14 claim that its own institutional records are independently verified merely because TA14 produced them?
2. Is integrity distinguished from independence?
3. Can the reader determine who produced material evidence?
4. Can the reader determine where independent verification terminates?
5. Can the reader determine when independence was not established?
6. Does TA14 claim to have solved the general attestation-recursion problem?

---

## 16. Financial Questions

The Independent Reader should be able to answer:

1. What does registration cost?
2. What later activities may carry fees?
3. Is a registrant required to purchase later services?
4. Can a previously free activity be retroactively charged?
5. Are paid pathways disclosed before commitment?

A material fee question marked **NOT STATED** prevents an instrument governing that fee from passing final adoption.

---

# PART V — DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS

## 17. Comparison

After both reviews are complete, the Informed Review and Independent-Read Review are compared.

The comparison is not intended to determine which reader is more intelligent or knowledgeable.

It asks:

> **Did both readers derive materially compatible institutional meaning from the document itself?**

---

## 18. Divergence Classes

### D0 — NO MATERIAL DIVERGENCE

Both readings are materially compatible.

No substantive correction is required.

### D1 — EDITORIAL DIVERGENCE

The difference concerns wording, presentation, navigation, or minor clarity without materially changing rights, obligations, findings, or institutional meaning.

Correction is recommended.

### D2 — INTERPRETIVE DIVERGENCE

The readers reach different interpretations that could affect understanding of a material rule.

Correction is required before adoption.

### D3 — GOVERNANCE DIVERGENCE

The readers disagree about a material right, obligation, authority, limitation, publication condition, withdrawal consequence, evidence state, finding meaning, or financial obligation.

The instrument fails the adoption gate.

### D4 — HIDDEN-CONTEXT DEPENDENCY

The intended interpretation is understandable only because the informed reader possesses context not contained in the instrument.

The instrument fails the adoption gate.

---

# PART VI — CORRECTION

## 19. Correct the Instrument, Not the Reader

When divergence occurs, TA14 Authority should not resolve the problem merely by explaining the intended meaning to the Independent Reader.

Doing so contaminates the independent-read condition without correcting the instrument.

The preferred response is:

**Identify divergence → Correct instrument → Issue revised candidate → Repeat independent read**

---

## 20. Revision Cycle

A material correction should create a new candidate revision.

Example:

**Candidate 1.0-R1**

The corrected instrument should then be tested again against the failed question or, where the revision materially affects multiple provisions, against the full question set.

---

# PART VII — ADOPTION GATE

## 21. Minimum Adoption Standard

A material governance instrument may pass the Independent-Read Adoption Gate when:

- no D3 Governance Divergence remains;
- no D4 Hidden-Context Dependency remains;
- no material question is INTERNALLY CONFLICTED;
- required financial terms are stated where applicable;
- material rights and obligations are not IMPLIED ONLY;
- material non-claims are explicit;
- material limitations are attached to the propositions they limit.

---

## 22. Possible Validation Outcomes

### VALIDATED FOR ADOPTION

The instrument satisfies the minimum standard.

### VALIDATED WITH EDITORIAL CORRECTIONS

No material governance defect remains, but minor clarity improvements are recommended.

### REVISION REQUIRED

Material ambiguity or interpretive divergence remains.

### FAILED — HIDDEN CONTEXT

The intended meaning still depends materially on information outside the instrument.

### NOT REVIEWABLE

The review record is insufficient to determine whether the instrument passed.

---

# PART VIII — VALIDATION RECORD

## 23. Required Validation Record

Each completed validation should preserve:

- instrument ID;
- candidate version;
- review date;
- informed-review status;
- independent-read status;
- question responses;
- identified ambiguities;
- divergence classifications;
- corrections made;
- final disposition;
- final adopted version.

The record should not require publication of a reviewer's private personal information.

---

## 24. Validation Does Not Establish Legal Sufficiency

Independent-read validation establishes only that the instrument passed the defined TA14 readability and interpretation-control process.

It does not establish:

- legal enforceability;
- statutory compliance;
- contractual sufficiency;
- regulatory approval;
- absence of legal ambiguity;
- fitness for every jurisdiction.

Legal review is a separate function.

---

# PART IX — PORTABLE QUALIFICATION CONTROL

## 25. Findings Must Carry Their Material Qualifications

Independent-read validation must examine whether a material finding can be copied, quoted, exported, displayed, or otherwise transported without the limitation necessary to interpret it.

Where separation would materially strengthen or distort the finding, the design should be corrected.

The governing rule is:

> **A material qualification must travel with the finding it qualifies.**

---

## 26. No Blank Provenance

Where evidence provenance materially affects interpretation, an empty value is not acceptable.

Use an explicit state such as:

**NOT REPORTED**

**NOT SUBMITTED**

**NOT PRESERVED**

**NOT INDEPENDENTLY ESTABLISHED**

**OUTSIDE REVIEW SCOPE**

A reader should not be required to guess what a blank means.

---

# PART X — TA14 SELF-APPLICATION

## 27. TA14 Instruments Are Reviewable

TA14 Authority should apply this protocol to its own material governance instruments.

Institutional authorship does not create an exemption.

TA14 should be willing to discover that:

- its language is ambiguous;
- a right is unstated;
- a limitation is separable;
- a fee is unclear;
- a withdrawal rule is incomplete;
- a finding can be misquoted;
- institutional intent exists only in institutional memory.

Discovery of such a condition is a reason to improve the instrument.

It is not a reason to conceal the review result.

---

## 28. Recursion Boundary

This protocol does not claim that TA14 Authority can independently validate its own independence merely by applying a TA14 protocol to itself.

The protocol provides a method for exposing interpretation dependency.

It does not eliminate the broader attestation-recursion problem.

Where independent review terminates, that boundary should remain visible.

---

# PART XI — CORE RULES

## 29. Record Rule

**If the document does not say it, private memory should not be required to supply it.**

---

## 30. Reader Rule

**An informed reader is not a substitute for an independent reader.**

---

## 31. Divergence Rule

**Materially different reasonable readings are evidence of a document defect.**

---

## 32. Correction Rule

**Correct the instrument before educating the reader.**

---

## 33. Qualification Rule

**A material limitation must travel with the result it limits.**

---

## 34. Silence Rule

**A blank must not be allowed to imply the strongest interpretation.**

---

## 35. Adoption Rule

**No material hidden-context dependency. No authoritative adoption.**

---

# PART XII — INITIAL APPLICATION

## 36. First Candidate Instrument

The first planned application of this protocol is:

**TA14 Registration & Evidence Terms — TA14-RET-001**

The validation should specifically test whether an outside reader can independently determine:

- what registration asserts;
- what registration does not assert;
- ownership and IP boundaries;
- treatment of closed implementations;
- evidence submission boundaries;
- publication rights;
- retention;
- withdrawal;
- historical preservation;
- corrections;
- participant disagreement;
- version succession;
- fees;
- evidence provenance;
- TA14's own independence boundary.

TA14-RET-001 should not be represented as having passed Independent-Read Validation until the validation has actually occurred and the resulting record has been preserved.

---

# Closing

TA14 Authority asks governance systems to preserve evidence strongly enough that another party does not have to reconstruct what happened from private memory.

Its own governance instruments should meet the same standard.

A rule that exists only in the minds of the people who wrote it is not yet a sufficiently preserved institutional rule.

The document must be able to carry its own meaning.
